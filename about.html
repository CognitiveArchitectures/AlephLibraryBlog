You read something that moves you. You highlight it. Six months later, the idea is still there—but you don't know where.

Your library shouldn't be a list of finished books. It should be everything you've ever thought—interwoven, alive, and at your fingertips.

Four core ideas.
Not a hundred features.

Aleph does a few things with severe intention—each one in service of a single thesis: reading should compound.

01

Active Harvesting

Highlight, annotate, even draw by hand. Every idea stays tied to its book, its page, and the question it raised. Nothing dies in a margin.

02

A Graph, Not a List

Relate ideas across books, with the author as the axis. Your reading becomes a network that grows with you—not a graveyard of finished titles.

03

Provable Reading

Not "marked as read." Verified reading sessions, and ideas that come back to you, show what you actually read—and what stayed.

04

On-Device Intelligence

Apple Intelligence, on device, recaps what you've read so far, so you can pick up any book where you left it. Private by design—nothing leaves your device.

Questions

Questions readers ask

Is it like Goodreads?

Goodreads records which books you read. Aleph keeps what they left you: your highlights, your notes and the hours you actually read. You can use both.

And Kindle or Apple Books?

They’re good for buying and reading, but your highlights stay there. Aleph reads your own PDF and EPUB files (without DRM), and you can export your reading.

Does it use AI?

Only on your device, with Apple Intelligence on compatible iPhones and iPads, to recap where you left off. It doesn’t write for you, doesn’t judge you and doesn’t send your books anywhere.

Where does my data live?

On your iPhone or iPad and in your own iCloud. We don’t have access to it. Others only see what you choose to publish in the community or on your public profile, and you can export your reading.

Android? Mac?

For now, iPhone and iPad.

What can I do without the app?

Share your favorite books as an image, read other readers’ profiles and blogs, and tell us what you think. All free, here on the site.

A letter

Why Aleph

In the beginning there was nothing. Then, an idea. And now Aleph is about to cross from the field of ideas into the world.

Reading and writing are a pillar of human society: they're how knowledge survives from one generation to the next. So isn't it strange how little the everyday experience of it has changed? For most of us, we still read the way we did in 2010: highlight, jot a note, and watch most of those notes drift into oblivion. A few tools have tried to change that, but they sit apart from your everyday reading, or turn it into work.

There's always a reason we do what we do. We read out of curiosity: to understand more, to answer our questions, to create and share, to become better. And yet, even in the digital era, this side of reading hasn't been given the focus it deserves. We have all the knowledge we could want within reach, but how much of it actually stays with us? How easy is it, really, to own, retain, connect, and assimilate what we read?

Lately a new kind of tool promises to do even the understanding for you: ask, and it hands back a summary, an answer, a digest. But knowledge you didn't work for slips away as fast as it arrived—understanding you didn't build was never really yours. Aleph refuses to think for you. It asks the opposite: to do the work yourself—to harvest an idea, wrestle with it, connect it, and say it back in your own words. The friction isn't a flaw to remove. It's where understanding is made.

It's not about which books you read. It's about which idea you want to understand.

Aleph is built with one core intention: to give you the tools to truly assimilate what you read into your own thinking. To make the knowledge yours, and to use it, whether to create something and add your grain of sand to the vast ocean of human knowledge, or simply to share it.

Because there's a conversation out there: a great one, between authors across centuries. And you can pull up a chair and take part. They aren't comparing which books they've read; they're wrestling with ideas. That's why you read, too: to appreciate, to understand, and to give shape to ideas of your own.

On December 1, Aleph sees its first sunrise on the App Store. It's built local-first, Apple-first and privacy-first: it works offline, and your library lives on your device and in your own iCloud, never mined by corporations to build a profile and sell you things. It's made to harvest, connect, and assimilate what you read, so your highlights and notes are never buried in oblivion, but within reach every time you connect a new idea. Aleph brings purpose to reading, helping you make the most of every session and truly own the knowledge in your personal library.

We'll be listening closely and answering as fast as we can, hoping to give you a way of reading worthy of this era: one that turns the device in your hand into something that helps you grow and reach what you set out to learn.
